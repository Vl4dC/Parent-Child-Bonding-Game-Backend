name: illustration
description: A picture of the thing itself, with no words in it. Use only where seeing it teaches more than labels would: a scene, a place, an object, an animal or plant, a moment in history (a volcano erupting, a coral reef, a Roman market, a butterfly's life on one leaf). The brief must describe the picture concretely: the subject, the setting, what is happening and from what viewpoint. Never for anything that needs labels, numbers, steps or arrows (use diagram or chart), and never for something to try (use interactive).
---
# How an illustration is drawn

No model writes code for this kind. The brief goes straight to the image model (`ILLUSTRATION_MODEL`),
wrapped in the house style in `visuals/illustrate.py`, so every picture in a lesson looks alike.

## What makes a good brief
- One subject, described as you would to an illustrator: "a green seedling pushing up through dark soil,
  its roots spreading down, seen from the side as a cut-away of the ground".
- Concrete and accurate: colours, shapes and proportions that are true to life.
- Nothing the picture would have to spell out. The caption and the lesson text carry the words.
